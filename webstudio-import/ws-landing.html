✦ what you'd actually use it for

Not another place to manage. One less thing to hold in your head.

You don't open Dexter to do more work. You open it because the alternative is carrying all of this yourself, across however many client projects you're running at once.

"A client's feedback lands at 11pm, unstructured."

Dexter

Paste it in. Dexter pulls out what actually changed, flags anything that shifts scope or the deadline, and adds it to the dossier — you wake up to a clear read, not a wall of text to re-parse.

"Wait — what did we actually agree to on this?"

Dexter

It's in the dossier, dated, in plain English — not somewhere in a thread from three weeks ago you'd have to go scroll back through.

"A client asks where things stand, mid-afternoon, no warning."

Dexter

The Briefing card already has the answer — on track or not, what's blocking it, what's next. You read it back. You don't write it from scratch.

"This file's been sitting here for weeks — why did I keep it again?"

Dexter

Every file is linked to the task or decision it came from. Open it and you get the context back, not just the file.

✦ how it actually works, no metaphors

Two lists, not one confusing one

Early builds tried sorting everything into a single board by status. It didn't hold up — your own to-dos and Dexter's suggestions aren't the same kind of thing, so now they live in two different places instead of one that pretends to be both.

✦ why not just use what you already have

You've probably tried gluing this together already

A task manager, a folder of files, maybe a chatbot for the odd summary. None of them know that this file is why that task exists, or that this decision is why the deadline moved. That connective tissue is the whole job here — not replacing any one of them.

What you haveWhat it actually does
Task managerTracks tasks — yours, not Dexter's, and not why they exist
File managerStores files, with no memory of what they meant at the time

Automation toolDoes exactly what you told it to, forever, whether it still makes sense or not
